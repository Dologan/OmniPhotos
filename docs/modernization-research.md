## Preserve the OmniPhotos baseline

OmniPhotos uses panoramas captured along an approximately circular camera trajectory. Proxy geometry and optical flow between neighboring frames help interpolate viewpoints and create translational parallax. A panorama taken while rotating about a stationary optical center provides little baseline for estimating depth. A circular trajectory also leaves some surfaces hidden, especially when the viewer moves away from the ring. Keep the same input frames, poses, proxy and renderer when comparing a new flow estimator. [OmniPhotos, Bertel et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3417770)

For the Insta360 ONE RS 1-Inch 360 Edition, retain the original dual-lens media and export a stitched **2:1 equirectangular** master from Insta360 Studio. Record export settings, timing and stabilization choices. Use consistent exposure and white balance where feasible. Walk a slow loop at roughly constant height. Check that a heading or stabilization change can be accounted for when registering camera poses. Inspect close railings, moving subjects and objects crossing the stitch. A stitched panorama approximates a central camera, and the dual-lens separation can leave near-field stitching errors even when scene depth is correct. Preserve the full-resolution export for comparisons. [Insta360 ONE RS 1-Inch 360 Edition](https://www.insta360.com/product/insta360-oners/1inch-360)

## What changed after the review

The import path checks for 2:1 panoramas and preserves source-frame indices through extraction and projection. The new profile estimates poses from one perspective crop per panorama and checks connected-model coverage before cache generation. DIS remains the flow baseline. The setup guide recommends a seated Quest Link check and records the paired tests required before adopting a new flow estimator, geometry prior or representation.

Sections 3.2 and 6 of the paper clarify two boundaries for those comparisons. The authors used DIS and FlowNet2 and evaluated a scene-adaptive proxy fitted to a sparse reconstruction. The validated modernization profile uses PyCOLMAP and DIS with the optional adaptive proxy fitting disabled. Compare against a separate adaptive-proxy run when aiming to reproduce the paper's visual results. Section 6.1 describes the original experience as 5-DoF: arbitrary head rotation and translation in the capture plane. Validate horizontal translation within the capture ring and score vertical motion separately as a limitation. [OmniPhotos paper, sections 3.2, 4, and 6](https://richardt.name/publications/omniphotos/OmniPhotos-BertelEtAl-SIGAsia2020-paper.pdf)

## Where a new method could pay off

Start with one representative capture loop from the Insta360 and one loop with a known difficulty, such as a blank wall, nearby railings or a troublesome stitch. Check the stitched export, camera registration and seated translation in Quest Link. Fix defects with a clear capture or configuration cause first. If the current workflow produces usable poses and a stable view within the target time and memory budget, keep it. The fixture smoke test exercised the pipeline, while this check would establish the first target-hardware baseline.

### Pose recovery with stella_vslam

**Why consider it.** PyCOLMAP currently sees one perspective crop per panorama. If that crop faces a blank wall or contains mostly moving subjects, the rest of the sphere may still contain stable features. stella_vslam supports an equirectangular camera model directly. Access to the remaining directions could recover lost frames, improve loop closure, and reduce manual crop changes or recaptures. [stella_vslam](https://github.com/stella-cv/stella_vslam) · [COLMAP](https://github.com/colmap/colmap)

**Trade-off and decision.** Adding a SLAM backend means building and maintaining an adapter for timestamps, source frame IDs, lost tracks, camera-to-world conventions, and monocular scale. Whole-sphere tracking can also pick up motion or stitching errors that the current crop avoids. Defer the adapter unless repeated failures leave useful static features outside the crop and a change of crop heading still gives unreliable coverage. If that happens, compare against the best practical PyCOLMAP crop. Look for fewer pose gaps and less manual recovery, with consistent loop closure and no regressions on the representative loop. Check pose quality as well as registration count. Multiple crops from one panorama share an approximate camera center and cannot supply new translation baselines by themselves.

### Correspondence with SEA-RAFT and RAFT

**Why consider them.** DIS is a fast CPU baseline that already fits the viewer’s flow format. Learned correspondence could improve matches where DIS produces visible doubling or flicker, especially around thin objects or weak texture. RAFT is the reference architecture for this hypothesis. SEA-RAFT predicts an initial flow to reduce refinement work, and its paper reports efficiency and cross-dataset accuracy gains on conventional flow benchmarks. That makes a small SEA-RAFT model the first candidate if recurring flow artifacts justify GPU inference. RAFT is a reference if a later comparison needs to explain a result, so there is little reason to integrate both up front. [RAFT, ECCV 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12039) · [SEA-RAFT, ECCV 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14793)

**Trade-off and decision.** Full-resolution all-pairs correlation over a panorama is an expensive starting point. Overlapping perspective faces or tiles reduce the working set and introduce overlap fusion, edge and longitude-wrap handling. That work can create seams or add enough preprocessing time to erase the gain. Try one small SEA-RAFT pilot only if the artifact persists with credible poses and a reasonable proxy, and the underlying stitch is sound. Fix frames, poses, geometry and renderer for the comparison. Convert matched endpoints to rays, use periodic longitude and report both angular cycle error and valid coverage. Adopt only if held-out stereo views show less doubling or flicker and the measured runtime and peak VRAM fit the desktop workflow. A better flow score alone leaves the viewer benefit unproven.

### Proxy fitting with Depth Anything V2

**Why consider it.** Accurate camera poses can still leave the sparse point cloud thin on flat or texture-poor surfaces. A relative-depth estimate could add constraints where the proxy has little support, reducing warping under head translation. Depth Anything V2 is a candidate for that particular gap because it can predict dense depth from a perspective view. [Depth Anything V2, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)

**Trade-off and decision.** An inferred edge or depth discontinuity can be wrong, and scale can drift between crops or frames. The prior needs multi-view alignment across overlaps, explicit depth conventions and confidence masking. Even a good depth map can exceed what the existing smooth proxy can represent. Defer until a separate run with the paper’s adaptive proxy shows a persistent error that correlates with missing geometry. If that condition holds, try the prior on the affected regions and use held-out views to check whether boundaries warp less without adding temporal drift. Keep the prior optional and stop if sparse geometry cannot anchor its scale.

### Replacing the renderer with 3D Gaussian Splatting

**Why it came up.** For a static scene with sufficient multi-view coverage, splats can carry more view-dependent appearance and fine structure than a smooth proxy. They could be useful if the product needs a wider viewing volume and capture can supply additional viewpoints. [3D Gaussian Splatting, SIGGRAPH 2023](https://repo-sam.inria.fr/fungraph/3d-gaussian-splatting/)

**Trade-off and decision.** This would replace major parts of reconstruction, assets and rendering. A single ring still undersamples disocclusions and height changes. Training and stereo rendering also introduce new memory and frame-time costs, especially if standalone Quest becomes a target. Keep splatting out of the current evaluation queue. Revisit it only if wider or vertical motion becomes a requirement and a revised capture plan covers those views. A modern representation alone offers little justification for replacing an established seated workflow.

## Evidence needed for a decision

1. Identify the recurring failure and the cause the candidate is meant to address. Set the acceptance criteria before running a comparison, including the visible improvement, tolerated regressions, preprocessing time and memory budget. Start with one affected scene and one representative scene. Stop if the candidate cannot clear the practical budget or shows no improvement in the target artifact.
2. Withhold frames on the ring with temporal buffers and capture a separate, registered holdout inside the ring for horizontal translation. Compare identical angular viewports at the same exposure and color transform. Score edge doubling, flicker, seams and missing coverage separately. For full-panorama PSNR, weight squared error by the cosine of latitude. Measure LPIPS on perspective views sampled approximately uniformly by solid angle, and report masked and unmasked results. [LPIPS, CVPR 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03924)
3. Confirm any visible gain on wired Quest Link with stereo, horizontal translation, recentering and sustained rendering. Record dropped frames and p50, p95 and p99 CPU/GPU frame times. The full display interval is 13.89 ms at 72 Hz and 11.11 ms at 90 Hz, with part of that budget reserved for the runtime. Evaluate Air Link separately if wireless performance becomes important.
